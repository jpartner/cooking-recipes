# Focaccia — 24h Caputo Nuvola Super

24-hour cold-fermented focaccia in teglia using Caputo Nuvola Super Type 0 (W290–320). Sized for a 25x35cm pan — crispy on the outside, open and airy inside.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo Nuvola Super (Type 0) | 285g |
| Water (room temp) | 228g (80% hydration) |
| Salt | 6g (2%) |
| Olive oil (in dough) | 15g (5%) |
| Caputo dried yeast | 0.5g |
| Olive oil (for pan and topping) | generous — at least 30ml |

### Sourdough variant

Replace the dried yeast with **57g active sourdough starter** (100% hydration) and adjust:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo Nuvola Super (Type 0) | 256g |
| Water (room temp) | 199g |
| Salt | 6g |
| Olive oil (in dough) | 15g |
| Sourdough starter (100% hydration, active) | 57g |

Totals remain the same — 285g flour, 228g water, 80% hydration.

### Topping

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Flaky sea salt | a few pinches |
| Fresh rosemary | a few sprigs, leaves picked |
| Olive oil | drizzle |

## Method

### 1. Mix

Dissolve the yeast (or add the starter) in the water. Add the flour and mix until no dry patches remain. Rest for 20 minutes (autolyse). Add the salt and olive oil, then mix until incorporated — the dough will be very wet and sticky. That's correct at 80% hydration.

### 2. Stretch and fold (1.5 hours)

Do 3 sets of stretch-and-folds at 30-minute intervals. Wet your hands, grab one side of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over the top. Rotate and repeat on all four sides — that's one set. The dough should get noticeably smoother and more cohesive with each set. Leave it alone after the third set.

### 3. Cold ferment (24 hours)

Cover tightly and straight into the fridge. The slow, cold fermentation develops flavour and builds the open, irregular crumb structure.

### 4. Pan and proof (3–4 hours before baking)

Generously oil the 25x35cm pan — don't be shy, the oil is what fries the bottom and edges. Tip the cold dough straight into the pan. Gently stretch it toward the corners but don't force it — if it springs back, leave it for 15 minutes and try again.

Cover and leave at room temperature for 3–4 hours. The dough should fill the pan, look puffy, and jiggle when you nudge it.

### 5. Dimple and top

Oil your fingers well. Press straight down through the dough to create deep dimples all over — these hold pools of olive oil and create the characteristic texture. Drizzle generously with olive oil, scatter the rosemary and flaky salt.

### 6. Bake (18–22 minutes)

Oven to 240°C. Bake until the top is golden and the edges are deeply crispy. The bottom should be crunchy — if it looks pale when you lift a corner, give it a few more minutes.

## Notes

- **Nuvola Super vs Cuoco:** Nuvola Super (W290–320, Type 0) is engineered for this — big irregular bubbles, light airy crumb. Caputo Cuoco (W300–320, Type 00) also works well but gives a slightly chewier crumb and crispier crust. Same recipe, same hydration, same timing — only the flour changes. Worth a side-by-side test if you have both.
- **Hydration:** 80% is the sweet spot. If you're comfortable with wet dough, push to 85% for an even more open crumb. Below 75% and you lose the characteristic airiness.
- **Olive oil:** use more than you think. The pan should be generously pooled, not just coated. The oil fries the dough on contact and creates the crispy golden bottom and edges that make focaccia what it is.
- **Don't knead.** Stretch-and-folds are gentler on the gluten and preserve the gas bubbles that become the open crumb. Kneading pushes them out.
- **Dimpling:** go deep, right to the bottom of the pan. Timid dimples puff back up and you lose the texture contrast.
- **Scaling to 30x40cm pan:** multiply everything by 1.4 — 400g flour, 320g water, 8g salt, 0.7g yeast, 20g olive oil.
