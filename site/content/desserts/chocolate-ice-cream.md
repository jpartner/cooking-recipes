# Chocolate Ice Cream

Custard-based chocolate ice cream, deepened with cocoa and melted dark chocolate so it still tastes of chocolate after freezing. Makes about 1 litre.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | In Poland | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Whole milk | Mleko 3,2% | 400ml |
| Double cream | Śmietanka kremówka 30% or 36% | 300ml |
| Egg yolks | Żółtka | 5 |
| Caster sugar | Cukier drobny, or zwykły cukier biały | 110g |
| Dutch-process cocoa powder | Kakao (niesłodzone) — not kakao rozpuszczalne | 35g |
| Dark chocolate (70%), finely chopped | Czekolada gorzka 70% | 100g |
| Fine salt | Sól | ¼ tsp |
| Vanilla extract | Ekstrakt waniliowy or pasta waniliowa | 1 tsp |

## Method

### 1. Bloom the cocoa

Put the cocoa in a bowl with about 100ml of the cold milk. Whisk to a smooth, lump-free paste. This stops the cocoa clumping when it hits the hot pan.

### 2. Make the custard base

Whisk the 5 egg yolks with 110g caster sugar and the salt until pale and slightly thick.

Pour the cocoa paste, the rest of the milk, and the cream into a saucepan. Heat until just steaming — not boiling.

Pour the hot milk mixture slowly into the egg yolks, whisking constantly. Return everything to the pan over low heat and cook, stirring with a spatula, until the custard coats the back of a spoon and reads **82–84°C** on a thermometer. Don't let it boil or it will scramble.

### 3. Melt in the chocolate

Take the pan off the heat. Add the chopped chocolate and leave it for 1 minute, then whisk until completely smooth. Stir in the vanilla.

### 4. Chill

Strain through a fine sieve into a bowl — this catches any cooked egg and the last cocoa lumps. Press cling film directly onto the surface. Refrigerate for **at least 4 hours**, ideally overnight. The colder the base, the better it churns.

### 5. Churn

Taste the chilled base. It should taste slightly too sweet and clearly of chocolate — both sweetness and cocoa dull once frozen. Adjust with a pinch more salt or a teaspoon of sugar if it needs it.

Pour into your ice cream maker and churn according to the machine's instructions — usually 25–35 minutes. It's done when it holds soft-serve consistency.

Transfer to a freezer-safe container, press cling film onto the surface, and freeze for **at least 4 hours** to firm up.

### 6. Serve

Remove from the freezer 10–15 minutes before serving. Good on its own, or next to the chocolate fondant while that is still warm.

## Notes

- **Chocolate:** 70% is the sweet spot. Milk chocolate tastes bland and oversweet once frozen. Above about 80% the ice cream sets harder and tastes harsh — drop the chocolate to 80g and add 15g sugar.
- **Caster sugar** is white sugar with finer crystals than ordinary granulated, so it dissolves into the yolks. In Poland that is **cukier drobny**, sometimes labelled "do wypieków". Diamant and Südzucker both sell it. Regular **cukier biały** works — whisk for an extra minute. Do not use **cukier puder** (icing sugar).
- **Dutch-process cocoa** is cocoa powder treated with an alkali, so the natural acidity is neutralised. It is darker (deep brown, not reddish) and tastes rounder and less sharp than "natural" cocoa. The packet may say alkalised, European-style, or *kakao alkalizowane* / *o obniżonej kwasowości*. In Poland you do not need a special import: plain unsweetened **kakao** from DecoMorreno, Wawel, or Delecta is already this kind. Natural cocoa is uncommon on Polish shelves and works here, but the ice cream will taste sharper. Do not buy **kakao rozpuszczalne** or drinking chocolate — those are sweetened and will throw the custard off.
- **Cream:** UK double cream is about 48% fat. **Śmietanka 36%** is the one to buy; 30% works, and the ice cream is a little less rich.
- **No ice cream maker?** Pour the chilled base into a shallow container and freeze. Whisk vigorously (or blitz in a food processor) every 45 minutes for 3–4 hours until set.
- **Egg whites:** this uses yolks only, so you will have 5 whites. Save them for meringues or freeze for later.
- **Keeps:** 2 weeks in the freezer before ice crystals start to develop.
