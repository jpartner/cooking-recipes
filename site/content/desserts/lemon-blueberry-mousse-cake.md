# Lemon Blueberry Mousse Cake

A lemon sponge base under a thick blueberry mousse, with lemon whipped cream and fresh blueberries on top. Adapted from [Stephanie's Sweets](https://stephaniessweets.com/lemon-blueberry-mousse-cake/) for a **32cm springform tin with a 7cm ring**, roughly twice the area of the original 23cm tin.

The original sets its mousse with American instant pudding mix and freeze-dried blueberries, and neither is easy to find in Poland. Here the mousse is blueberry purée, mascarpone and whipped cream, set with gelatine. Everything on the shopping list is normally stocked at Biedronka.

Serves 20–24. Make it the day before: the mousse needs **at least 6 hours** in the fridge, and overnight is better.

**Layers in the tin:** about 2.5cm of sponge and about 3.5–4cm of mousse. The topping goes on after the ring comes off.

## 1. Lemon sponge

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mąka tortowa (cake flour) | 330g |
| Skrobia ziemniaczana (potato starch) | 15g |
| Baking powder | 1½ tsp (7g) |
| Bicarbonate of soda | ½ tsp (2g) |
| Salt | ¾ tsp |
| Butter, soft | 65g |
| Rapeseed oil | 110ml |
| Fine sugar | 300g |
| Lemon zest | 3 lemons |
| Lemon juice | 45ml (3 tbsp) |
| Eggs L, room temperature | 3 |
| Śmietana 18%, room temperature | 90g |
| Maślanka (buttermilk), room temperature | 180ml |

### Method

1. Heat the oven to **175°C** (165°C fan). Grease the tin, line the base with baking paper, and grease the paper.
2. Sift the flour, starch, baking powder, soda and salt together.
3. Beat the butter, oil, sugar and lemon zest on high for 2–3 minutes, until pale.
4. On medium speed, beat in the lemon juice, then the eggs one at a time, then the śmietana.
5. On low speed, add the dry ingredients in three goes, alternating with the buttermilk, and stop as soon as it's just combined. Finish by hand with a spatula, scraping the bottom for dry pockets.
6. Spread level in the tin and bake for **25–32 minutes**, until golden and a skewer comes out clean. The layer is thinner than in a 23cm tin, so start checking at 25 minutes.
7. Cool in the tin for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool completely.
8. Wash and dry the tin, line the base with fresh baking paper, and put the cooled sponge back in. If it has domed, trim the top flat with a long serrated knife.

## 2. Lemon syrup (optional, recommended)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lemon juice | 60ml (about 2 lemons) |
| Sugar | 40g |
| Water | 40ml |

Warm everything until the sugar dissolves, then let it cool. Brush it over the sponge in the tin. It keeps the base moist and adds a sharper lemon note under the mousse.

## 3. Blueberry mousse

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Frozen blueberries | 600g |
| Sugar | 100g |
| Lemon juice | 2 tbsp |
| Gelatine | 40g |
| Cold water, for the gelatine | 150ml |
| Mascarpone, cold | 500g (2 tubs) |
| Cukier puder (icing sugar) | 80g |
| Vanilla | 1 sachet of vanilla sugar or 1 tsp extract |
| Śmietanka 30–36%, very cold | 1 litre (2 cartons) |

### Method

1. **Purée:** simmer the blueberries, sugar and lemon juice for 8–10 minutes, until the berries burst. Blend smooth with a stick blender. Sieve it if you want a perfectly smooth, seedless mousse. You should have about 450–500ml.
2. **Gelatine:** sprinkle the gelatine over the 150ml of cold water and leave it for 10 minutes to swell. Stir it into the **hot, not boiling** purée until fully dissolved, with no grains left. Let it cool to room temperature, about 25°C. It should be cool to the touch but still liquid. If it starts to set, warm it gently.
3. **Cream:** whip the cold cream to **soft peaks**, so it holds a shape but still flops over. Don't take it to stiff peaks, or it won't fold in smoothly.
4. **Mascarpone:** in a large bowl, beat the cold mascarpone with the cukier puder and vanilla for about 30 seconds, only until smooth. Overbeating makes it grainy.
5. Whisk 3–4 spoonfuls of the purée into the mascarpone to loosen it, then whisk in the rest of the purée.
6. Fold in the whipped cream in three goes, gently, until there are no white streaks.
7. Pour the mousse over the sponge in the tin and level it. Tap the tin gently on the counter to release air bubbles.
8. Cover and chill for **at least 6 hours**, ideally overnight.

## 4. Lemon whipped cream and decoration

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Śmietanka 30–36%, very cold | 500ml |
| Mascarpone, cold | 250g |
| Cukier puder (icing sugar) | 60g |
| Lemon zest | 1–2 lemons |
| Vanilla | 1 sachet of vanilla sugar or 1 tsp extract |
| Fresh blueberries | 250g |

1. Run a thin knife, dipped in hot water and wiped dry, around the inside of the ring. Release the ring and lift it off.
2. Beat the mascarpone, cukier puder, vanilla and zest until smooth. Add the cream and whip together to **medium-stiff peaks**. The mascarpone makes it stable enough to hold for a day.
3. Spread a thin layer over the top, then pipe rosettes around the edge with a star nozzle, or swirl the rest on with a spoon.
4. Pile the fresh blueberries in the middle and finish with a little lemon zest.

## Timeline

- **Day before:** bake the sponge in the morning and cool it for about 1½ hours. Make the mousse and pour it in by early afternoon, then chill overnight.
- **On the day:** unmould, top, and decorate up to 4 hours before serving. Keep it in the fridge.

## Notes

- **Will it fit?** About 6.5cm of cake and mousse in a 7cm ring leaves a little headroom. If your sponge rises higher than 2.5cm, trim it, or hold back some mousse and serve it in glasses.
- **Gelatine:** 40g sets about 3 litres of mousse firmly enough to slice cleanly once the ring is off. For a softer, spoonable mousse, use 30g and serve it straight from the fridge.
- **Instant gelatine:** if you buy instant gelatine (*żelatyna błyskawiczna*), follow the packet. It usually dissolves straight into warm liquid without soaking.
- **Śmietan-fix** (Dr. Oetker) can stand in for the mascarpone in the topping: whip 500ml cream with 2 sachets and the sugar. It's lighter but less stable.
- **Colour:** the mousse comes out lilac-purple from the berries alone, so no food colouring is needed.
- **Cream fat:** 30% whips fine if it's very cold. 36% is more forgiving and gives a firmer topping.
- **Keeps:** 3 days covered in the fridge. It doesn't freeze well once topped. Before topping, it freezes fine for a month: thaw it overnight in the fridge.
