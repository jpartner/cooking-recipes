# Margherita

Classic margherita — San Marzano tomato sauce, fior di latte, fresh basil, and olive oil. Enough topping for 4 x 12-inch pizzas.

## Ingredients

### Sauce

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tinned tomatoes (whole, peeled) | 400g (1 tin) |
| Salt | 1/2 tsp |
| Garlic | 1 small clove, finely grated (optional) |

No cooking — crush the tomatoes by hand or blend briefly until slightly chunky. Stir in the salt and garlic if using. That's it.

### Topping

| Ingredient | Amount (per pizza) |
|---|---|
| Sauce (from above) | ~80g |
| Fior di latte, torn | 80–100g |
| Fresh basil leaves | 4–5 |
| Extra virgin olive oil | a good drizzle |
| Parmesan, finely grated | optional — light dusting |

## Method

### 1. Prep the mozzarella

Tear the fior di latte into rough pieces 20 minutes before baking and leave on kitchen paper. This draws out excess moisture — wet mozzarella pools and makes the base soggy.

### 2. Sauce the base

Spoon ~80g of sauce onto the shaped dough. Spread with the back of the spoon in a spiral from the centre outward, leaving the rim clean. Keep it thin — too much sauce and the centre won't cook through.

### 3. Add the cheese

Scatter the torn fior di latte evenly, leaving gaps. It will spread as it melts — if you cover every inch now, the pizza drowns.

### 4. Bake

Onto the hot cast iron plate at 270°C for 5–7 minutes. Grill/broiler at the end to blister the cheese.

### 5. Finish

Straight out of the oven: fresh basil leaves, a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, and parmesan if you like.

## Notes

- **Tomatoes:** San Marzano are worth it here — they're sweeter and less acidic than standard tinned tomatoes. Don't cook the sauce. Raw keeps it bright and fresh against the heat of the oven.
- **Fior di latte vs fresh mozzarella di bufala:** bufala has more flavour but much more moisture. If using bufala, tear it up further in advance and drain it well, or the pizza will be wet. Fior di latte is more forgiving.
- **Less is more.** A margherita with too much sauce or cheese is worse than one with too little. You should see patches of sauce between the cheese after baking.
- **Basil goes on after baking.** On top of the raw pizza it just blackens and turns bitter.
