# Pizza Dough

22-hour pizza dough using Pulcinella Type 0 (W260) flour. Makes 4 balls at ~290g each — enough for 4 x 12-inch pizzas. Long cold retard for flavour, baked at 270°C on a cast iron plate.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pulcinella Flour 0 (W260) | 700g |
| Water (room temp) | 441g (63% hydration) |
| Salt | 21g (3%) |
| Olive oil | 14g (2%) |
| Caputo dried yeast | 1g |

### Sourdough variant

Replace the dried yeast with **140g active sourdough starter** (100% hydration) and adjust the flour and water to account for what the starter contributes:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pulcinella Flour 0 (W260) | 630g |
| Water (room temp) | 371g |
| Salt | 21g |
| Olive oil | 14g |
| Sourdough starter (100% hydration, active) | 140g |

Totals remain the same — 700g flour, 441g water, 63% hydration.

## Method

### 1. Mix

Dissolve the yeast (or add the starter) in the water. Add the flour and mix until no dry patches remain — a shaggy mass is fine. Rest for 20 minutes (autolyse). Add the salt and olive oil, then knead or fold until the dough is smooth and passes a basic windowpane test — 8–10 minutes by hand, 5–6 in a stand mixer on low.

### 2. Bulk ferment (2–3 hours)

Place in the proving container at 22–24°C. Give it a set of stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes for the first 1.5 hours (3 sets total), then leave it alone. The dough should look puffy and feel airy by the end — roughly 50% volume increase.

### 3. Ball up

Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Divide into 4 equal pieces (~290g each). Shape each into a tight ball — pull the edges underneath and pinch the seam, then cup your hands around the ball on the work surface and drag it toward you to build tension on the top. Place seam-side down in a lightly oiled container or on a tray with space between each ball. Cover tightly.

### 4. Cold retard (16–18 hours)

Straight into the fridge. This is where the flavour develops — the slow, cold fermentation gives complexity you can't get from a same-day dough.

### 5. Warm up (1.5–2 hours before baking)

Pull the dough balls out of the fridge and leave at room temperature, still covered. They should be visibly puffed and feel pillowy-soft when ready. Don't rush this — cold dough won't stretch properly and will spring back.

### 6. Preheat

Put the cast iron plate on the middle shelf and preheat the oven to 270°C for at least 45–60 minutes. The plate needs to be fully saturated with heat.

### 7. Shape and top

Working one ball at a time: press from the centre outward with your fingertips, leaving a 1–2cm rim untouched. Pick up and gently stretch over your knuckles, letting gravity do the work. Don't use a rolling pin — it kills the air in the rim.

Top quickly. Less is more — overloaded pizza steams instead of baking.

### 8. Bake (5–7 minutes)

Slide the pizza onto the hot cast iron plate. Bake until the base is charred in spots and the rim is puffed and golden. In the last minute, switch to grill/broiler on high to blister the top.

## Notes

- **Flour:** Pulcinella Type 0 W260 peaks at ~18 hours fermentation. The 22-hour schedule (2–3h bulk + 16–18h cold + 2h warm-up) sits comfortably in its range.
- **Hydration:** 63% is a good starting point for this flour. If the dough feels easy to handle after a few bakes, push to 65% next time for a more open crumb.
- **Yeast amount:** 1g dried yeast is very little — that's deliberate. The long cold retard does the work. More yeast risks over-proofing in the fridge.
- **Sourdough timing:** sourdough is slower to get going, so the bulk ferment may run closer to 3 hours. Judge by volume and feel, not the clock. Make sure the starter is at peak activity (doubled, domed, passes the float test) before mixing.
- **Cast iron plate:** 10mm cast iron holds serious heat. The first pizza will bake fastest — after that, give the plate 5 minutes between pizzas to recover.
- **Shaping:** if the dough tears, it's either under-proofed or too cold. Let it rest 10 minutes and try again.
- **Same-day shortcut:** if you only have 8–10 hours, increase the yeast to 2g, bulk ferment for 1.5 hours at 24°C, ball up, and cold retard for the remaining time. Less flavour but still decent.
