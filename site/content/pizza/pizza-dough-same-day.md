# Pizza Dough — 5h Same-Day Pulcinella W260

Same-day pizza dough using Pulcinella Type 0 (W260) flour. Makes 4 balls at ~290g each — enough for 4 x 12-inch pizzas. No cold retard — everything happens at room temperature. Less complex than the overnight versions but still a proper dough with good texture.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pulcinella Flour 0 (W260) | 700g |
| Water (warm, ~30°C) | 441g (63% hydration) |
| Salt | 21g (3%) |
| Olive oil | 14g (2%) |
| Caputo dried yeast | 3g |

## Schedule

| Step | Duration | Clock (if starting at noon) |
|---|---|---|
| Mix + autolyse | 30 min | 12:00 |
| Bulk ferment | 1.5h | 12:30 |
| Ball up + room temp proof | 2h | 14:00 |
| Preheat oven | 1h (overlaps with proof) | 15:00 |
| Shape, top, bake | 30 min | 16:00 |
| Eating | — | ~16:30 |

## Method

### 1. Mix

Dissolve the yeast in the warm water — the higher water temperature speeds up yeast activity. Add the flour and mix until no dry patches remain. Rest for 15 minutes (autolyse). Add the salt and olive oil, then knead until smooth — 8–10 minutes by hand, 5–6 in a stand mixer on low. The dough should be soft and pass a basic windowpane test.

### 2. Bulk ferment (1.5 hours)

Place in the proving container at 24–26°C — a warm spot in the kitchen is fine. Give it a set of stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes (3 sets total). The dough should roughly double in volume and feel airy and gassy by the end.

### 3. Ball up

Divide into 4 equal pieces (~290g each). Shape into tight balls — pull the edges underneath and pinch the seam, then cup your hands around the ball and drag toward you to build surface tension. Place seam-side down in a lightly oiled container or on a tray with space between each ball. Cover tightly.

### 4. Room temp proof (2 hours)

Leave the balls at room temperature (24–26°C), covered. They should roughly double in size, feel pillowy-soft, and spring back slowly when poked. If they spring back fast, give them more time. If they don't spring back at all, they're over-proofed — bake them sooner.

### 5. Preheat

Put the cast iron plate on the middle shelf and preheat the oven to 270°C for at least 45–60 minutes. Start this about 1 hour before you plan to bake — it overlaps with the proof.

### 6. Shape and top

Press from the centre outward with your fingertips, leaving a 1–2cm rim untouched. Stretch gently over your knuckles, letting gravity help. The dough may be slightly less extensible than overnight versions — if it fights back, rest it for 5 minutes and try again.

Top quickly and lightly.

### 7. Bake (5–7 minutes)

Slide the pizza onto the hot cast iron plate. Bake until the base is charred in spots and the rim is puffed and golden. Grill/broiler on high in the last minute to blister the top.

## Notes

- **Warm water:** 30°C water gives the yeast a head start. Don't go above 35°C — that starts to stress the yeast.
- **More yeast:** 3g vs 1g in the overnight recipe. Necessary because there's no long cold ferment — the dough needs to be fully proofed in ~3.5 hours total.
- **Flavour:** same-day dough won't have the same depth as a 22h or 48h ferment. A pinch of sugar (5g) in the dough can help with browning but isn't essential.
- **Warm room:** if your kitchen is cool (<22°C), proof the dough in the oven with just the light on, or in a microwave with a cup of hot water.
- **If you have 6–7 hours:** reduce the yeast to 2g and let the bulk ferment run to 2 hours. The slower ferment gives slightly better flavour.
