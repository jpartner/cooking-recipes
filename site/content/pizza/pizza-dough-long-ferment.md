# Pizza Dough — 48h Pizzuti 00 W310

48-hour pizza dough using Molini Pizzuti Type 00 Costa d'Amalfi flour (W310). Makes 4 balls at ~460g each — enough for 4 x 15-inch pizzas. The long, cold fermentation develops complex flavour and an airy, digestible crust.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Molini Pizzuti Type 00 Costa d'Amalfi (W310) | 1060g |
| Water (cold, from the fridge) | 689g (65% hydration) |
| Salt | 32g (3%) |
| Olive oil | 21g (2%) |
| Caputo dried yeast | 0.8g |

### Sourdough variant

Replace the dried yeast with **160g active sourdough starter** (100% hydration) and adjust:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Molini Pizzuti Type 00 Costa d'Amalfi (W310) | 980g |
| Water (cold, from the fridge) | 609g |
| Salt | 32g |
| Olive oil | 21g |
| Sourdough starter (100% hydration, active) | 160g |

Totals remain the same — 1060g flour, 689g water, 65% hydration.

## Method

### 1. Mix

Dissolve the yeast (or add the starter) in the water. Add the flour and mix until no dry patches remain. Rest for 30 minutes (autolyse) — the higher-protein flour benefits from the extra hydration time. Add the salt and olive oil, then knead until smooth and elastic — 10–12 minutes by hand, 6–8 in a stand mixer on low. The dough should be soft, slightly tacky, and pass a windowpane test.

### 2. Bulk ferment (2 hours)

Place in the proving container at 22–24°C. Give it a set of stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes for the first 1.5 hours (3 sets), then leave it. You're looking for a 40–50% volume increase — don't push it further, the cold retard does the real work.

### 3. Ball up

Divide into 4 equal pieces (~460g each). Shape into tight balls with good surface tension. Place seam-side down in a sealed plastic container with space between each ball. Lightly oil the container or dust the balls with semolina to prevent sticking.

### 4. Cold retard (44–46 hours)

Straight into the fridge at 3–5°C. The dough will rise slowly over the first 24 hours, then the flavour development really kicks in during the second day.

### 5. Warm up (2 hours before baking)

Pull the dough balls out and leave at room temperature, still covered. They need longer than a shorter-ferment dough to wake up — they should be visibly puffed, jiggly, and room temp throughout before you shape them.

### 6. Preheat

Cast iron plate on the middle shelf, oven to 270°C for at least 45–60 minutes.

### 7. Shape and top

Handle gently — long-fermented dough is more delicate. Press from the centre outward with your fingertips, leaving the rim untouched. Stretch carefully over your knuckles. The dough should be extensible and cooperative — if it fights back, give it 10 more minutes.

Top lightly and bake immediately.

### 8. Bake (5–7 minutes)

Onto the hot cast iron plate. The rim should puff dramatically — the long ferment creates bigger, more irregular bubbles. Grill/broiler at the end to blister the top.

## Notes

- **Cold water:** use fridge-cold water. The dough will warm up during mixing and bulk ferment — starting cold keeps it from fermenting too fast before it hits the fridge.
- **0.5g yeast:** this is tiny — about 1/4 teaspoon. With 48 hours of fermentation, you need very little. Too much and the dough over-proofs in the fridge, collapses, and tastes yeasty. A digital scale that reads to 0.1g is useful here.
- **Hydration:** 65% works well with Type 00 W310 — the stronger gluten handles the extra water and the long ferment. If you're comfortable, push to 67% for an even lighter result.
- **Sourdough timing:** the bulk ferment may need an extra 30–60 minutes since sourdough is slower to get going. Use less starter (100g vs 140g in the short recipe) to avoid over-proofing during the long cold retard.
- **Fridge temperature matters.** 3–5°C is ideal. If your fridge runs warm (6°C+), the dough may over-proof — check at 36 hours and bake earlier if the balls have flattened or smell strongly acidic.
- **72-hour extension:** reduce the yeast to 0.5g (or starter to 110g) and keep everything else the same. The dough will be even more flavourful but more fragile — handle very gently when shaping. Check at 60 hours for signs of over-proofing.
- **Sealed container:** the sealed plastic container prevents the dough balls from skinning over during the long fridge stay. No need to oil the surface or cover with cling film.
